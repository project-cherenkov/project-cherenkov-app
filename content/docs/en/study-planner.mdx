The study planner is optional. Everything else on the site works without an account.

## Accounts

You can sign up with an email address and password, and with Google when the deployment has Google sign-in configured. The **Account** page shows your name, email, sign-in methods and role:

- **Inchoatus** — you are signed in but do not have edit access.
- **Primus Inter Pares** — you are signed in and have edit access to the site's content tools.

You sign out from the Account page.

## How planning works

The planner is built for **revising** the OSN syllabus: it assumes you already know a lot of it and tries to spend your time where it helps most.

1. **Settings** — choose your subject, the OSN stage you are targeting (regency/city, province or national), your exam year and how many hours a week you can study. All stages use the same syllabus; they differ in difficulty.
2. **Self-ratings** — rate each syllabus topic from 1 to 5. Leaving a topic unrated means "no opinion".
3. **Plan** — the planner combines your settings, ratings and quiz answers into a schedule.

## What the plan contains

- **Confirmation quizzes first.** Self-assessment is a weak signal, so the planner asks you to confirm what you say you know.
- **Study sessions** ranked by how much you are likely to gain per hour.
- **Spaced reviews** timed backwards from your exam date.
- **A buffer before the exam**, with a final mixed pass.

The page shows today's tasks, anything overdue, what is up next and the coming weeks. If your weekly hours are not enough to fit everything, the plan says so plainly instead of pretending.

Study and review sessions are ticked off by hand. Quizzes complete their confirmation item automatically.

## Things to know

- Regenerating a plan keeps the items you have already completed.
- "Today" means your calendar day (Asia/Jakarta by default), not the server's.
- Some inputs are still placeholders and are not yet calibrated on real students, including the target mastery levels and any exam dates for 2027 that are projected from 2026. Treat the plan as a well-reasoned guide, not a guarantee.
- Informatics and astronomy currently have few quiz questions, so many of their topics cannot be confirmed by quiz yet.
