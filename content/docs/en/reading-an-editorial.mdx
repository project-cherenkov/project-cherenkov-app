Every editorial follows the same shape, so once you have read one you know how to read them all.

## The anatomy of an editorial

1. **The hook** — one sentence that tells you what is surprising or non-obvious. This is the same hook used on Instagram.
2. **The problem** — the concrete question being answered.
3. **The idea** — the key insight, stated before any heavy algebra.
4. **Try it** — the interactive visualization. Change the inputs, step through it, break it.
5. **The full proof** — the rigorous argument, with all the steps.
6. **Tags** — short labels you can use to find related editorials.

The visualization sits between the idea and the proof on purpose: the aim is for you to *see* the claim before you read the argument for it.

## Principle and error type

Near the top of every editorial you will see two labels:

- **Principle** — the general idea the editorial teaches, such as a technique that applies well beyond the one problem.
- **Error type** — the specific mistake the editorial corrects, when there is one.

Both are free text for now, because the vocabulary is still settling as more editorials are published. They are also how the archive filters work, so clicking through related principles is a good way to find more material on the same idea.

## Mathematics

Formulas are typeset with KaTeX at build time, so they appear instantly and do not reflow after the page loads. If you use a screen reader, the formulas are exposed as accessible MathML as well as the visual rendering.

## Reading with the syllabus

Editorials and materials can be linked to a syllabus topic. When they are, the editorial or material page links back to that topic, and the topic's row in the syllabus links forward to them. Use the syllabus as a table of contents and the editorials as the deep dives.
