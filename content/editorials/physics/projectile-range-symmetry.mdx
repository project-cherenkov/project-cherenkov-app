## The problem

Launch a projectile at $30°$ and it lands some distance away. Launch it
again, same speed, at $60°$ instead — and it lands in *exactly the same
spot*. This isn't a coincidence specific to $30$ and $60$: for any angle
$\theta$, launching at $\theta$ and at $90° - \theta$ gives the same range.
Why?

## The idea

Range depends on launch angle only through $\sin(2\theta)$, and
$\sin(2\theta) = \sin(180° - 2\theta) = \sin\big(2(90° - \theta)\big)$ —
the sine function's own symmetry does all the work. Two angles that are
complementary ($\theta$ and $90° - \theta$) produce the same $2\theta$
value reflected across $90°$ inside the sine, and $\sin$ treats those two
inputs identically.

Drag the angle slider below $45°$ and above it and watch: the range
markers line up for complementary pairs, but the *shape* of the flight
doesn't — a low, fast, flat arc versus a high, slow, steep one land at the
same point through very different paths.

## Full proof

**Setup.** Launch speed $v_0$, launch angle $\theta$, no air resistance,
flat ground. Position at time $t$:

$$x(t) = v_0 \cos\theta \cdot t, \qquad y(t) = v_0 \sin\theta \cdot t - \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2$$

**Time of flight.** Landing is $y(t) = 0$ for $t > 0$:

$$t = 0 \quad\text{or}\quad t = \frac{2 v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$$

so the nonzero flight time is $T = \dfrac{2 v_0 \sin\theta}{g}$.

**Range.** Substituting $T$ into $x(t)$:

$$R(\theta) = v_0 \cos\theta \cdot T = \frac{2 v_0^2 \sin\theta\cos\theta}{g} = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$$

using the double-angle identity $2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \sin(2\theta)$.

**Symmetry.** Compare $R(\theta)$ and $R(90° - \theta)$:

$$R(90° - \theta) = \frac{v_0^2 \sin\big(2(90° - \theta)\big)}{g} = \frac{v_0^2 \sin(180° - 2\theta)}{g}$$

and since $\sin(180° - x) = \sin(x)$ for any $x$:

$$\sin(180° - 2\theta) = \sin(2\theta) \implies R(90° - \theta) = R(\theta) \qquad \blacksquare$$

**Where it breaks in practice.** The error this editorial is filed under —
treating equal range as equal trajectory — shows up whenever a problem asks
something about the *flight itself* (max height, flight time, impact speed
vertically) rather than just where it lands. Those quantities are **not**
symmetric under $\theta \leftrightarrow 90° - \theta$: max height scales
with $\sin^2\theta$, which is not equal for complementary angles unless
$\theta = 45°$. Same landing spot, different everything else along the way
— the sandbox above makes this visible by showing the whole arc, not just
the endpoint.
